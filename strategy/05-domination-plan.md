# What Domination Actually Costs

Supersedes `04-90-day-plan.md` **if funded**. That plan was built for $500–1,500/mo,
which is a survival budget. This one answers a different question.

---

## Ruthless truth #1 — you cannot dominate "Australian IT"

Nobody can. Telstra can't. The market is tens of thousands of buyers, hundreds of
competitors, and no amount of money buys the whole thing.

Domination is only ever available in a **defined segment**. The entire strategy
hinges on choosing one narrow enough to own and large enough to be worth owning.
Everything else is spray.

## Ruthless truth #2 — you already have a wedge and you're not using it

Look at who does what:

| Competitor type | Managed IT | Cyber | Surveillance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical MSP | ✅ | ✅ | ❌ |
| Security integrator | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ |
| **Alphalogix** | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |

MSPs don't touch cameras. Security integrators can't configure M365. You do both,
and you're certified across five surveillance platforms *and* a Microsoft and
CrowdStrike partner.

**The buyer who needs all three is the multi-site operator** — retail chains,
hospitality groups, warehousing and logistics, franchise networks, clubs, schools,
strata. One buyer. Three budgets. One relationship.

That's not a marketing angle. It's a structural advantage almost nobody in this
market can match, and it should be the entire positioning.

### The beachhead

**NSW multi-site operators, 20–200 staff, 2+ locations.**

Retail, hospitality, logistics, franchise, clubs. Sydney metro first, then greater NSW.
Not "Australian businesses". Not "SMBs". This.

## Ruthless truth #3 — the segment is small enough to saturate

This is the number that makes domination real rather than aspirational.

- NSW businesses matching that profile: roughly **8,000–15,000**
- Decision-makers in them (owner, ops manager, GM, facilities): roughly **20,000–30,000**

To be the name that comes to mind, you need roughly **12–15 impressions per person
per month**, sustained. Do the arithmetic:

```
25,000 people  ×  15 impressions      =  375,000 impressions/month
375,000 impressions  ÷  1,000         =  375 CPM units
375  ×  $28 avg AU B2B CPM            =  ~$10,500/month
```

**~$10,000/month buys genuine saturation of this segment.** Not "some presence" —
saturation. Every target buyer sees Alphalogix 3–4 times a week, every week.

That is what domination costs. It is a real, defensible number, and it is far less
than most people assume, *because the segment is narrow.* Widen the segment to
"Australian business" and the same saturation costs $200k+/month. That's the whole
argument for the beachhead.

## Ruthless truth #4 — you're underspending because you're using the wrong maths

Most MSPs judge ad spend against monthly revenue. That's the error. Judge it
against **lifetime value.**

**One managed services client:**

```
30 seats × $130/seat/month        =  $3,900 MRR  =  $46,800/year
× 4-year average tenure           =  $187,000 revenue
× ~45% gross margin               =  ~$84,000 gross profit
```

**One multi-site surveillance deployment:** $25,000–80,000 project, ~35% margin
= $9,000–28,000 gross profit, plus recurring monitoring.

**Cross-sold (the wedge):** land one, sell the other. **$100,000+ gross profit per logo.**

So: you can spend **$15,000–20,000 acquiring a single client** and still be
comfortably ahead. Most MSPs won't spend $2,000 — which is precisely why this market
is winnable. Your competitors are bringing a $1,500/month budget to a fight where the
prize is $100k per customer.

**Spending $10k/month to win 2–3 clients a month is not aggressive. It's arithmetic.**

## Ruthless truth #5 — you're missing the channel where buying happens

Nothing so far touches **Google Search**, and that's the biggest hole in the plan.

Meta and LinkedIn are *demand creation* — interrupting people who weren't looking.
Google Search is *demand capture* — someone typing "managed IT services sydney" or
"CCTV installation for retail" is in-market **today**, with budget, comparing vendors.

Australian B2B CPCs run **$4–8**, with competitive MSP terms higher. Prices are up
31% since January and 21% year on year while search volume fell 23% — the market is
getting more expensive and more contested. Every month of delay costs more than the last.

High-intent terms to own:

*Capture (buy now):* managed IT services sydney · IT support sydney · CCTV
installation sydney · security cameras for business · Essential Eight compliance ·
Microsoft 365 migration sydney

*Displacement (competitor pain — the highest-value cluster you can buy):*
"IT provider not responding" · "change IT provider" · "IT support review" ·
"MSP contract exit"

That second cluster is where a brand built on honesty wins outright. Someone
searching "how to change IT provider" has *already decided*. They just need
somewhere to land.

## What each budget actually buys

### Tier 1 — Present · $3,000/month ($36k/yr)

| Line | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Google Search (capture) | $1,500 |
| Meta (creation + retargeting) | $1,000 |
| Landing pages, CRM, tooling | $500 |

**Result:** 25–40 leads/mo, 6–10 qualified, **1–2 new clients/mo from month 4.**
You're in the game. You are not dominating anything.

### Tier 2 — Serious contender · $10,000/month ($120k/yr) ← **recommended**

| Line | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Google Search | $3,500 |
| Meta (prospecting + retargeting) | $2,500 |
| LinkedIn (now viable) | $2,000 |
| Video / content production | $1,500 |
| Tooling, CRM, landing pages | $500 |

**Result from month 4:** 100–140 leads/mo, 25–35 qualified, **3–5 new clients/mo.**

This is the saturation number from Ruthless Truth #3. At this level every target
buyer in the beachhead sees you weekly.

**12-month projection at 3/mo, conservative:**
```
36 clients × $3,900 MRR   =  $140,000 MRR  =  $1.68M ARR
Spend                     =  $120,000
```
Even at half that hit rate it pays for itself several times over — and MRR compounds,
while spend doesn't.

### Tier 3 — Own the segment · $25,000–30,000/month ($300–360k/yr)

Everything in Tier 2, plus outbound SDR, industry events and sponsorships, PR and
earned media, YouTube pre-roll, and a **dedicated marketing hire** (~$110–130k).

**Be honest about this tier:** it is not a budget problem, it is a *headcount*
problem. Above roughly $15k/month you need someone whose full-time job this is.
Money without an owner burns.

## Recommendation

**Tier 2. $10,000/month. 12-month commitment. NSW multi-site beachhead.**

Not because it's the middle option — because it is the exact number that saturates
the chosen segment, and because the LTV maths makes it low-risk. One 30-seat client
returns $84k gross profit against $120k of annual spend. **Three clients in twelve
months and the whole programme is paid for.** Realistic is 25–40.

### Non-negotiables at this level

1. **12 months, not 3.** Months 1–3 are learning. Judging this in week 6 wastes
   the entire investment. If you can't commit 12, stay at Tier 1.
2. **Somebody owns speed-to-lead.** Under 5 minutes in business hours. At $10k/month
   a slow callback is the single most expensive failure available to you.
3. **Google Search goes live first.** It captures existing demand and pays back
   fastest. Meta and LinkedIn build the demand Google then captures.
4. **Pick the beachhead and hold it.** The instinct at month 3 will be to widen
   because "we could also do professional services". Widening is how you go from
   dominating a segment to being invisible in a market.

---

## The gap you need to close

You've asked to dominate on a budget of $500–1,500/month. Those two things are not
compatible, and no strategy makes them compatible.

$1,500/month buys presence in one channel. Domination of the beachhead costs
~$10,000/month. **The strategy is right either way — the difference is purely how
fast it works and whether competitors get there first.**

The decision isn't which strategy. It's how much you're willing to fund.

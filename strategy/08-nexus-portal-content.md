# Nexus (Client Portal) — How to Publish It

**What it is:** a client portal / dashboard for existing Alphalogix clients.
**What it's for:** MSP leads, not portal signups.

---

## ⚠️ Before publishing: confirm the demo data is genuinely synthetic

**Good news:** the recording is of `demo.alphalogix.com.au`, not the production
environment at `nexus.alphalogix.com.au`. That removes most of the risk.

**Still worth 30 seconds of checking**, because demo environments are very often
clones of production with the login changed rather than purpose-built with invented
data. Scrub through once looking for:

- Real client or company names in lists, tickets or asset records
- Staff names and email addresses
- Ticket subjects and contents
- Hostnames, serial numbers, IP addresses, tenant IDs
- Security posture or open vulnerabilities attributable to a named client

For a cyber security company, a real client's data appearing in a marketing video is
catastrophic — commercially, contractually, and under the Privacy Act. Blurring is not
sufficient; a paused frame is a screenshot.

If it's all invented data, publish with confidence. If anything real appears, re-record
that section against seeded dummy data.

## The reframe: this is not a product launch

A cold audience does not care about your portal. No business owner has ever thought
*"I wish my IT provider had a nicer dashboard."* Nobody has ever switched MSP because
of a UI.

So publishing this as "Introducing Nexus" will get very little traction. The product
name means nothing to anyone outside the company, nobody searches for it, and it asks
the viewer to care about your thing before you've made them care about their problem.

**But as proof, it's outstanding.**

Every competitor *claims* transparency. You can **show** it:

> "Here is the actual dashboard our clients log into. Your tickets. Your assets. Your
> security posture. Your spend. Any time you want, without asking us."

That is the honesty positioning made physical. It's the difference between saying
you're transparent and proving it on screen. Nothing else in the content library
does that as directly.

**So don't publish a product launch. Publish evidence for a claim you're already making.**

## The hook

Lead with the tension, never the product name.

| Hook | Why |
|---|---|
| **"Ask your IT provider for this and watch what happens."** ★ | Best of the set. It's a challenge the viewer can act on today, and it makes them evaluate their current provider — which is exactly the thought that precedes a switch. |
| "Most IT providers won't show you this." | Confession framing, consistent with the ad set. |
| "You shouldn't have to ask what you're paying for." | Leads on the grievance, not the feature. |
| "This is what your IT should look like." | Weakest — it's about the tool, not the viewer. |

The product name appears **once**, late, as a label. Never in the first three seconds.

## The cut hierarchy

One recording, five assets:

| # | Asset | Length | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Full walkthrough | 4–8 min | YouTube · Tech Tuesday · website |
| 2 | The core proof | 45–60s | LinkedIn, FB, IG native |
| 3 | Vertical cutdown | 15–20s | Reels, Stories |
| 4 | Single-feature clips ×3 | 10–15s | Organic filler, one feature each |
| 5 | Retargeting cut | 30s | **Meta retargeting — see below** |

**Critical for watch time: your face has to be in it.** A silent screen capture with a
moving cursor is the lowest-retention format there is. Either picture-in-picture with
Peter narrating, or open on Peter for 5 seconds before the screen share. Screen
recordings without a human present die in the first two seconds.

## Where it actually belongs in the ad plan

**Not as cold creative. As retargeting.**

The sequence that converts:

```
Cold audience  →  the intro ad        (hook: "most IT companies won't tell you this")
Warm audience  →  the portal demo     (proof: "here's what we show our clients")
                                       ↓
                                  free 15-min review
```

Someone who has already seen the intro ad and then sees the portal is being shown
evidence for a claim they've already heard. That's a far stronger second touch than
repeating the intro ad at them.

**Concretely:** in `../ad-copy/02-ten-dollar-test-runbook.md`, the portal cut becomes
the creative for the video-viewers-50%+ retargeting audience — alongside or instead of
Script 05.

## Distribution order

1. **Peter's LinkedIn first.** Highest-quality audience for this by a wide margin, and
   his personal profile out-reaches the company page. Post it as "we built this because
   clients kept asking what they were paying for."
2. **YouTube long-form** the same day — the anchor everything else links to.
3. **Company LinkedIn** mirrors it.
4. **FB and IG native 45–60s** the following day. Native, not a link to YouTube.
5. **Reels/Stories cutdowns** across the following fortnight.
6. **Add to the retargeting pool** once the ad account is live.

## The offer to attach

Don't ask for a portal signup — nobody outside your client base can use it. Ask for
the thing it proves you'd be good at:

> **"Book a free 15-minute review — and we'll show you the portal properly."**

The portal becomes a reason to take the meeting, which is exactly the job you want it
doing.

## Don't let this split focus

The paid programme is about to launch on a $10/day test with one message. A portal
demo published as a second, separate narrative would divide a small amount of attention
across two stories.

Published as *proof for the existing story*, it doesn't split anything — it makes the
one story stronger. Keep it in that lane.
